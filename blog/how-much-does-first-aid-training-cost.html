First aid training costs in the UK vary from under £40 per person to over £300, depending on the qualification level, provider, and delivery format. For employers trying to budget accurately, the key distinction is between open public courses (you send individuals to a venue) and on-site group training (the trainer comes to you). For businesses with more than three or four staff to train, on-site group training is almost always significantly cheaper per head.

Emergency First Aid at Work (EFAW) Cost

EFAW is a one-day (6-hour) Level 2 qualification suitable for most offices, retail environments, schools, and lower-hazard workplaces. It covers CPR, AED use, choking, bleeding, burns, and shock. The certificate is valid for 3 years.

First Aid at Work (FAW) Cost — 3-Day Course

The full First Aid at Work qualification is a Level 3 award spanning three days (18 hours). It is required for higher-hazard workplaces — construction, warehousing, manufacturing, and any site with significant physical risks. Paediatric First Aid Cost

Paediatric First Aid is a separate two-day qualification required by Ofsted for all staff in nurseries, after-school clubs, childminders, and settings caring for children under 5. It is not a substitute for EFAW and vice versa — if your childcare staff need both, they must complete both courses.

What Factors Affect the Price You Pay?

1. Course level and duration: EFAW (1 day) costs less than FAW (3 days), which costs less than combined or specialist courses. This is the biggest single factor.

2. Open vs on-site: Open public courses charge per person — typically £110–£400 depending on qualification. On-site group training charges a flat fee for the group, making it much cheaper per head when you have four or more delegates.

3. Location and travel: Some providers charge travel on top of the course fee, particularly for remote locations. Skills 42U includes travel within Kent and the South East in the group price — no surcharges for reaching sites across Medway, Maidstone, Dartford, or further into Kent.

4. Accreditation body: All HSE-compliant first aid qualifications must be Ofqual-regulated. Be wary of very cheap courses that are not regulated — they will not satisfy an HSE inspection or Ofsted visit.

5. VAT: Ofqual-regulated training is VAT-exempt under HMRC rules. Some providers add VAT to ancillary services (materials, admin fees) even when the training itself is exempt — check what is included in any quote you receive.

How to Get the Best Value — Practical Tips for Employers

  • Fill the group: On-site training with Skills 42U costs £495 regardless of whether you send 4 or 12 delegates. If you can pull together a full group — combining different teams or departments — the cost per person drops dramatically.
  • Stagger renewals sensibly: If you have 24 staff with certificates expiring at different dates, two full groups trained 18 months apart is cheaper than sending individuals to open courses on a rolling basis.
  • Don't over-train: Not every employee needs a full First Aid at Work certificate. A correct needs assessment — something we provide free as part of any enquiry — ensures you buy the right course, not the most expensive one.
  • Check your insurer: Some commercial insurers offer a small premium discount for businesses with documented, up-to-date first aid provision. Ask your broker whether holding Ofqual-regulated certificates affects your policy.
