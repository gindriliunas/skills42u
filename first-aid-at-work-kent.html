Why Kent's Higher-Hazard Sites Need FAW

The HSE standard for ports, logistics and industrial Kent

Not every Kent workplace can rely on the 1-day EFAW certificate. The Health and Safety (First Aid) Regulations 1981 require employers to match their first aid provision to the hazards their workforce actually faces — and Kent carries a distinct hazard profile driven by its ports, quarries and cross-Channel freight routes. The Port of Dover is the busiest ferry port in Europe, running a constant flow of HGVs, freight handling and marine operations, and the Eurotunnel terminal at Folkestone adds another concentrated freight and logistics hub on the same coastline. Inland, the Dartford and Ebbsfleet distribution parks feed the M25/A2 corridor, the Medway towns retain a strong marine engineering and manufacturing base, and quarrying and aggregates operations run across the Kent Downs. All of these sectors typically sit above the threshold where EFAW alone is sufficient.

Under the CDM Regulations 2015, first aid provision on notifiable construction projects must be in place before work starts, not arranged once a site is already live — a live issue for the large-scale build-out at Ebbsfleet Garden City and along the HS1 corridor. We deliver FAW at haulage depots, distribution units, marine workshops, quarry offices and welfare cabins right across Kent, timing sessions around shift patterns so training doesn't hold up operations.

⛑️ Everything in EFAW

CPR, AED use, unconscious casualty management, choking, bleeding control, burns and medical emergency recognition — the full EFAW syllabus, extended and reinforced over three days.

🦴 Fractures & Spinal Injury

Recognition and safe management of suspected fractures, spinal and head injuries — critical for quarrying, freight handling and construction sites.

🫁 Chest & Eye Injuries

Management of penetrating chest injuries and eye trauma, common risks around forklifts, marine workshops and aggregate handling.

☢ Poisoning & Exposure

Recognition and initial management of poisoning and chemical exposure — relevant for marine engineering, manufacturing and quarrying teams.

⌛ Prolonged Casualty Care

Extended casualty monitoring and care while awaiting South East Coast Ambulance Service — especially relevant on large port and quarry sites with longer response windows.

🛠️ Site-Specific Scenarios

Practical scenarios built around freight, plant, height and manual-handling incidents, not just office-based first aid situations.

Is FAW Right for Your Kent Business?

Who the 3-day FAW qualification is designed for

FAW is the right choice where the HSE's guidance identifies genuine higher hazards — machinery, working at height, hazardous substances, confined spaces or heavy manual handling — rather than the slips, trips and medical emergencies typical of an office. The HSE's first aid guidance requires the level of training to match your written needs assessment, not a default based on headcount.

FAW is typically required for:

  • Port & freight operations (Dover, Folkestone)
  • Logistics & distribution (Dartford, Ebbsfleet)
  • Quarrying & aggregates (Kent Downs)
  • Marine engineering & manufacturing (Medway)
  • Construction & site-based trades
  • Working at height or with plant
  • Large workforce (50+ employees)
  • Multiple shift patterns

EFAW is usually enough for:

  • Maidstone & Tunbridge Wells offices
  • Retail & Canterbury or Ashford high street shops
  • Restaurants & hospitality venues
  • Estate agents & letting offices
  • Hair & beauty salons
  • Gyms & leisure centres
  • Schools & nurseries (admin staff)
  • Small businesses (under 25 staff)

Many Kent businesses run a mixed workforce — an office team alongside a depot, quarry or production line. In these cases we typically recommend FAW for the site or operational staff and the shorter EFAW qualification for the office-based majority, delivered in the same visit. If your team also works with children or vulnerable people, our Paediatric First Aid Kent course covers that requirement separately.
