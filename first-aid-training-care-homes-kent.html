Why Care Homes Need Specialist First Aid Training

The dual requirement: HSE law and CQC standards

Kent has a large and growing care sector. From residential nursing homes in Maidstone and Medway, to dementia care facilities in Thanet and Canterbury, to supported living services across Ashford, Tonbridge, and Sevenoaks — care home operators across the county face a dual legal obligation on first aid that is more demanding than most other workplaces. Both the Health and Safety (First Aid) Regulations 1981 and the Care Quality Commission's Regulation 12 (Safe Care and Treatment) require evidence of adequate first aid provision — and the CQC will check it at every inspection.

The HSE classifies care homes as higher-hazard environments because of the vulnerability of residents, the physical nature of personal care work, and the frequency of medical emergencies in an aging residential population. Falls, choking, cardiac events, strokes, and diabetic emergencies are among the most common serious incidents in care settings — and they happen across all shifts, including nights, weekends, and bank holidays when ambulance response times are longer. This is why a first aid needs assessment for a care home almost always points to the full 3-day First Aid at Work (FAW) qualification for designated first aiders, rather than the 1-day EFAW.

Which Course Does Your Care Home Need?

FAW, Paediatric, or EFAW — matching the right course to each role

The right qualification depends on the nature of the care your home provides and the roles of the staff being trained. Here is a practical breakdown:

  • Residential and nursing homes for adults — designated first aiders should hold the full 3-day First Aid at Work (FAW) qualification. The FAW syllabus covers cardiac arrest, stroke recognition, diabetic emergencies, and seizure management — the medical emergencies most likely to occur with elderly or high-dependency residents. Some homes also train care assistants to EFAW level as a secondary layer, with FAW-qualified staff as primary responders on every shift.
  • Children's homes and residential care for under-18s — a Paediatric First Aid qualification is required for at least some staff. The Paediatric FA syllabus covers infant and child CPR, choking in young children, febrile convulsions, anaphylaxis, and poisoning — scenarios that are not adequately covered in adult-focused courses. Settings registered with Ofsted or CQC for children require this qualification by regulation.
  • Kitchen, domestic, and ancillary staff — EFAW is generally appropriate for staff who are not directly involved in personal care. It covers the core life-saving skills — CPR, AED, choking, and catastrophic bleeding — without the extended medical content of the 3-day FAW.

For a detailed breakdown of what is covered across both courses, read our guide on what happens on a First Aid at Work course. For a full breakdown of legal requirements specific to care settings, see our care home first aid requirements guide.

24-Hour Care and Shift Cover

Making sure your care home has first aid cover on every rota

The most common compliance gap in Kent care homes is not a lack of trained first aiders — it is first aid cover that exists during day shifts but breaks down for night shifts, weekends, and bank holidays. The HSE and the CQC are both explicit on this point: first aid provision must be adequate at all times that your home is operating — and a 24-hour residential care home is always operating.

For a care home running day, evening, and night shifts, this means training enough first aiders so that at least one FAW-qualified person is on duty at every point in the rota. When absences, annual leave, and sickness are factored in, most care homes with more than 20 staff need at least four to six FAW-qualified staff members to guarantee uninterrupted cover.

First aid certificates expire after three years, and there is no grace period. CQC inspectors will check certificate dates. We recommend building renewal dates into your HR system so that certificates never lapse. For guidance on timing renewals, see our post on how often first aid certificates need renewing.
