Why Manufacturing Sites Need Specialist First Aid Training

First aid in Kent manufacturing: what the law requires

Kent has one of the South East's strongest manufacturing bases. From the paper and packaging plants around Kemsley and Sittingbourne, to the food and drink manufacturers in Faversham and Swale, the pharmaceutical operations along the Medway corridor, and the defence and engineering businesses near Dartford and Gravesend — the county's industrial sector employs tens of thousands of people in environments where workplace injuries can be serious. The Health and Safety (First Aid) Regulations 1981 require every employer to provide adequate and appropriate first aid — for manufacturing businesses, that almost always means trained first aiders holding the full 3-day First Aid at Work (FAW) qualification.

The HSE classifies manufacturing as a higher-hazard sector. The combination of moving machinery, manual handling, electrical equipment, chemical exposure, and the pace of a production environment creates a meaningfully elevated risk profile compared to an office or retail workplace. The injuries most likely to require trained first aid response in a manufacturing setting — crush injuries, lacerations from cutting equipment, chemical burns, fractures, and machinery entanglement — are precisely the scenarios that the 1-day EFAW does not adequately prepare a first aider for. The 3-day FAW syllabus was designed with higher-hazard workplaces like factories and production floors in mind.

What the FAW Course Covers for Manufacturing

Why the 3-day FAW is right for most Kent production environments

The First Aid at Work syllabus covers the emergencies most likely to happen on a manufacturing or factory floor — not just the general medical scenarios covered in the shorter EFAW. Key areas relevant to manufacturing workers include:

  • Crush injuries — a critical skill when heavy plant, presses, or conveyor equipment is involved. The FAW covers recognition, safe management, and the specific risks of reperfusion injury after prolonged entrapment.
  • Fractures and dislocations — limb, rib, and pelvic fractures from falls or machinery contact are among the most common serious injuries in production environments. The FAW teaches immobilisation and safe casualty management.
  • Severe lacerations and catastrophic bleeding — cutting, stamping, and guillotine machinery creates risk of major blood loss. The FAW covers wound packing, tourniquet application, and haemostatic dressing use.
  • Burns and chemical exposure — relevant across food processing, pharmaceutical, chemical, and metal manufacturing. The FAW covers thermal burns, chemical burns, and eye irrigation protocols.
  • Head and spinal injuries — falls on factory floors, mezzanine levels, and during maintenance work can result in head trauma or suspected spinal injury. The FAW covers safe casualty management while awaiting the ambulance.
  • Medical emergencies — heart attack, stroke, diabetic emergency, anaphylaxis, and seizures occur in any workplace. The FAW covers these in greater depth than the EFAW.

Shift Working and First Aid Cover

Making sure your manufacturing site has cover on every shift

One of the most common compliance failures for Kent manufacturing businesses is first aid cover that exists on paper during day shifts but breaks down for evenings, nights, and weekends. The HSE's first aid guidance is explicit: first aid provision must cover all hours that your premises are occupied — not just core office hours.

For manufacturing businesses that run double shifts, continuous operations, or weekend production runs, this means training enough first aiders to guarantee cover on every rota. As a practical rule, we recommend that manufacturing businesses with three-shift patterns train at least one first aider per shift team — and that first aider should hold the full FAW, not just the EFAW.

First aid certificates are valid for 3 years, and there is no grace period if a certificate lapses. We recommend building certificate expiry dates into your HR or compliance calendar so you are never caught with an uncovered shift.
