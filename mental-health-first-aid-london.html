Why London Employers Need MHFA

Mental health is London's most common — and most overlooked — workplace risk

Mental health conditions are now the leading cause of workplace absence in the UK, and London's employment profile makes the capital particularly exposed. The HSE's 2023/24 statistics recorded 17.1 million working days lost nationally to work-related stress, anxiety and depression — more than physical injury and musculoskeletal disorders combined. London's long commutes, high cost of living, and concentration of high-pressure sectors such as finance, law, construction and hospitality mean many City, Canary Wharf and West End employers are managing this risk without any trained response in place.

A trained Mental Health First Aider is not a therapist or counsellor — they are a first point of contact: someone qualified to recognise the early signs of mental ill health, hold a supportive, non-judgemental conversation, and guide a colleague towards the right professional or organisational support. Exactly as a physical first aider keeps someone stable until the London Ambulance Service arrives, an MHFA helps a colleague through a crisis until professional help takes over.

What the 2-day Mental Health First Aider course covers

The full 2-day MHFA England course qualifies delegates as accredited Mental Health First Aiders. The curriculum covers:

  • The mental health continuum — understanding the spectrum from wellbeing to crisis
  • Depression & anxiety disorders — signs, symptoms and how to have a safe conversation
  • Psychosis — recognising the signs and responding safely
  • Suicide and self-harm — Safe Messaging guidelines, asking directly about suicidal thoughts, and what to do in an acute crisis

FAQs

Common questions about Mental Health First Aid in London

Mental Health First Aid (MHFA) is an internationally recognised training programme that teaches employees to recognise the signs of mental health conditions, provide initial non-clinical support, and guide colleagues towards appropriate professional help. It is not therapy or counselling — a Mental Health First Aider is a trained first point of contact, in the same way a physical first aider keeps someone stable until the London Ambulance Service arrives. Many London employers run MHFA alongside EFAW or First Aid at Work so their team has cover for both physical and psychological emergencies.
There is no UK law that mandates MHFA specifically, but the Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999 place a duty on every employer, including those across London, to assess and manage work-related stress risks. The HSE's Working Minds and Talking Toolkit guidance strongly recommends mental health first aid as a practical control measure. A growing number of London contractors, insurers and public-sector procurement frameworks now ask suppliers to evidence MHFA provision as part of tender requirements.
MHFA England recommends a minimum of one trained Mental Health First Aider per 50 employees, rising in high-pressure sectors such as finance, construction, healthcare and hospitality — all heavily represented across the capital. For London businesses with staff split across multiple floors, sites or boroughs, we typically recommend training at least one Mental Health First Aider per location so support is never more than a short walk away.
