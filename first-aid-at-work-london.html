Why London's Higher-Hazard Sites Need FAW

The HSE standard for construction, logistics and industrial London

Not every London workplace can rely on the 1-day EFAW certificate. Under the Health and Safety (First Aid) Regulations 1981, employers must match their first aid provision to the hazards their workforce actually faces — and for construction sites, warehousing, logistics depots, manufacturing units and facilities maintenance teams, that means the full 3-day First Aid at Work qualification. London's building boom, its position as the UK's largest logistics and distribution hub, and its dense network of maintenance and facilities contracts mean thousands of businesses across the capital carry this obligation, often alongside an office-based workforce that only needs EFAW.

Under the CDM Regulations 2015, first aid provision on notifiable construction projects must be in place from day one — a requirement that principal contractors and subcontractors across every London borough have to plan for before work starts, not retrofit once a project is underway. We deliver FAW at welfare cabins, depot offices and warehouse mess rooms across the capital, timing sessions around shift patterns so training doesn't hold up programme-critical work.

⛑️ Everything in EFAW

CPR, AED use, unconscious casualty management, choking, bleeding control, burns and medical emergency recognition — the full EFAW syllabus, extended and reinforced over three days.

🦴 Fractures & Spinal Injury

Recognition and safe management of suspected fractures, spinal and head injuries — critical for construction, height and heavy-plant environments.

🫁 Chest & Eye Injuries

Management of penetrating chest injuries and eye trauma, common risks on sites involving tools, machinery and airborne debris.

☢ Poisoning & Exposure

Recognition and initial management of poisoning and chemical exposure — relevant for facilities, cleaning and industrial workplaces.

⌛ Prolonged Casualty Care

Extended casualty monitoring and care while awaiting the London Ambulance Service — particularly relevant on large or remote sites with longer response windows.

🛠️ Site-Specific Scenarios

Practical scenarios built around plant, height and manual-handling incidents, not just office-based first aid situations.

Is FAW Right for Your London Business?

Who the 3-day FAW qualification is designed for

FAW is the right choice where the HSE's guidance identifies genuine higher hazards — machinery, working at height, hazardous substances, confined spaces or heavy manual handling — rather than the slips, trips and medical emergencies typical of an office. The HSE's first aid guidance requires the level of training to match your written needs assessment, not a default assumption based on headcount.

FAW is typically required for:

  • Construction & site-based trades
  • Warehousing & logistics depots
  • Working at height or with plant
  • Facilities & maintenance teams
  • Manufacturing & industrial units
  • Large workforce (50+ employees)
  • Multiple shift patterns
  • Remote or lone working roles

EFAW is usually enough for:

  • City & Canary Wharf offices
  • Retail & West End shops
  • Restaurants & hospitality venues
  • Tech & creative studios
  • Estate agents & letting offices
  • Hair & beauty salons
  • Gyms & leisure centres
  • Small businesses (under 25 staff)

Many London businesses have a mixed workforce — a warehouse or depot alongside an office-based sales or admin team. In these cases we typically recommend FAW for the site or maintenance staff and the shorter EFAW qualification for the office-based majority.
